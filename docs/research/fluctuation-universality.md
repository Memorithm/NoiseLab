# Fluctuation universality — Stage U0

## Research question

Does coarse graining cause fluctuation series generated by different mechanisms to approach a common statistical representation more strongly than expected from trivial one-point statistics alone?

This is a deliberately narrower and falsifiable form of the broader intuition that apparently unrelated noises may share deep structure. NoiseLab does **not** assume a common cosmological origin and does **not** treat visual similarity, a shared `1/f`-like spectrum, or a single matching exponent as proof of universality.

## Hypotheses

`H0` — after unit normalization, any observed cross-system convergence under coarse graining is explained by empirical marginals and generic averaging effects captured by independently shuffled surrogate controls.

`H1` — at least some independently generated fluctuation families exhibit multiscale convergence that exceeds those shuffled controls, indicating a candidate shared temporal/scaling structure worth stronger mechanistic tests.

Passing `H1` at Stage U0 produces only `SurrogateSeparatedCandidate`. It does not establish a universal law, common physical source, or cosmological ancestry.

## Fixed coarse-graining transformation

For a raw scalar series `x[0..n]`:

1. reject non-finite and zero-variance series;
2. standardize the full raw series to zero mean and unit population variance;
3. for each preregistered block size `b`, replace each non-overlapping block by its arithmetic mean;
4. discard only the incomplete trailing block and record that count;
5. require at least eight coarse samples at every tested scale.

The default Stage U0 scales are:

```text
1, 2, 4, 8, 16
```

Larger studies may extend this list, but confirmatory runs must freeze the scales before inspecting their outcomes.

## Multiscale descriptor

At every scale NoiseLab records:

- variance after raw-series standardization;
- lag-1 Pearson autocorrelation;
- population excess kurtosis;
- mean-centered sign-change rate.

The pairwise distance is fixed as the Euclidean norm of four transformed gaps:

```text
log variance ratio
lag-1 autocorrelation difference
bounded kurtosis difference: atan(kurtosis) / (pi/2)
sign-change-rate difference
```

The global trace also reports the least-squares slope of `log(variance)` versus `log(block size)`. For IID finite-variance noise under block means, `-1` is a known calibration target; it is not itself a universality criterion.

## Primary convergence statistic

For a pair of series A and B:

```text
convergence_score = distance(scale_first) - distance(scale_last)
```

A positive score means the selected representation became closer under coarse graining. A zero or negative score is retained as a negative result.

This endpoint is intentionally simple for Stage U0. Later stages may preregister richer trajectory-level statistics, but they must not silently replace this endpoint after seeing data.

## Surrogate null

For every repetition:

- independently Fisher-Yates shuffle A and B using pinned SciRust `SplitMix64`;
- preserve each empirical one-point distribution exactly;
- destroy temporal ordering;
- recompute the complete multiscale comparison.

The one-sided empirical randomization p-value uses the standard `+1` correction:

```text
p = (1 + count(surrogate_score >= observed_score)) / (R + 1)
```

The library requires at least 19 surrogates. The default is 199 with `alpha = 0.05`. Higher-stakes confirmatory runs should use substantially more repetitions.

Interpretation:

- `NoObservedConvergence`: score <= 0;
- `CompatibleWithShuffledNull`: score > 0 but `p > alpha`;
- `SurrogateSeparatedCandidate`: score > 0 and `p <= alpha`.

A candidate label means only that the selected temporal/scaling structure is not explained by this particular shuffled null.

## Stage U0 calibration

Before applying the test to heterogeneous physical systems, calibrate the machinery on synthetic controls already available through pinned SciRust:

1. independent Gaussian white-noise realizations — block-mean variance should scale approximately as `1/b` and the fitted exponent should approach `-1`;
2. Ornstein-Uhlenbeck realizations — preserve a known correlated stochastic process as a non-IID control;
3. identity comparison — identical inputs must have zero descriptor distance at every scale and must not be labeled as evidence of convergence;
4. seed reproducibility — surrogate distributions and decisions must reproduce exactly for fixed inputs/configuration.

These tests validate the harness, not the scientific conjecture.

## Stage U1 — same-domain scaling classes

Compare independently seeded and parameter-varied families within one mechanism:

- Gaussian white noise;
- Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes across correlation times;
- bistable Langevin fluctuations across declared regimes;
- FitzHugh-Nagumo subthreshold/spiking residuals where the observable is declared in advance.

The purpose is to determine whether the descriptor can distinguish known different temporal structures while recovering expected within-class similarities.

## Stage U2 — cross-mechanism NoiseLab systems

Only after U0/U1 calibration, compare fluctuation observables from mechanistically distinct systems already implemented in NoiseLab:

- driven damped oscillator residuals;
- semiconductor-laser relaxation residuals;
- bistable Langevin trajectories;
- FitzHugh-Nagumo fluctuations;
- attention/RoPE perturbation-response residuals when a scientifically meaningful scalar observable is preregistered.

For each source, preserve the raw series and provenance. The observable extraction rule must be frozen before cross-system comparison.

## Stage U3 — external physical data

A stronger universality claim requires external, provenance-preserving datasets from independent physical domains. Candidate domains include thermal/electronic fluctuations, photon-counting fluctuations, geophysical/environmental series, biological fluctuations, and cosmological fluctuation observables where licensing and measurement semantics permit reproducible ingestion.

External datasets must not be selected because they already resemble the desired answer. Dataset inclusion/exclusion, preprocessing, units, sample cadence, missing-data handling, and holdout families must be preregistered.

## Anti-false-positive controls

A convincing result must survive more than the Stage U0 shuffled null. Planned controls include:

- phase-randomized or spectrum-matched surrogates when a validated implementation is available;
- amplitude-distribution matched synthetic controls;
- time reversal where physically meaningful;
- window/start-offset sensitivity;
- scale-grid sensitivity;
- descriptor ablations;
- independent confirmatory source families not used to design the metric.

If convergence disappears under any scientifically necessary control, that negative result is part of the evidence record.

## Relation to Noise as Information

The universality program asks whether a fluctuation can contain two kinds of information simultaneously:

1. local information about the state or hidden degrees of freedom of its own system;
2. class-level information about a broader dynamical/scaling family.

The existing `Noise as Information` measurements should therefore be combined later with multiscale universality tests, rather than treating filtering as harmless preprocessing.

## Relevant methodological precedents

This program is informed by, but does not claim equivalence to, established renormalization/coarse-graining work including:

- Ferretti et al., *Phys. Rev. E* 105, 044133 (2022), renormalization of Gaussian stochastic processes;
- Lenggenhager et al., *Phys. Rev. X* 10, 011037 (2020), information-theoretic coarse graining;
- Dieball & Godec, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 129, 140601 (2022), coarse-grained fluctuation observables;
- current nonequilibrium work using renormalization-group fixed points and universal fluctuation scaling.

These references justify the methodology class. They do not establish the NoiseLab conjecture.
